# Algo Consistency Index — methodology (v0.2, live since 2026-10-26)

## What it is
A daily reading of whether the market is paying the price patterns retail FX/CFD algorithms trade, computed per mechanism family (trend/breakout, mean-reversion, session-clock) from public market data, validated against live algo panels, with every test pre-registered before it ran and every result, including failures, published here.

## Three layers
1. **Probe index (public backbone).** Twelve frozen textbook recipes (Donchian 20/10, opening-range breakout, M15 range break, EMA 20/50 cross, RSI(2), Bollinger fade, prior-day level fade, Asian-range fade, fixed-time London entry, post-fix drift, pre-rollover fade, random-entry control) replayed on raw ticks of seventeen symbols with the broker's published spread, the raw-feed spread excess around rollover and $4 per lot round-turn commission. Reading per probe and symbol: z-score over the trailing two years of the 20-day R per trade minus the random control's R per trade in the same exit geometry. Family index = mean of its legs, published only for a day on which every one of the seventeen symbols has a reading for every leg (from the 2026-11-05 row; change log below). Hypothetical, cost-included, never advertised as performance.
2. **Scoreboard (coincident).** Breadth, momentum z, realised drawdown load and SPRT kill count across live production accounts, labelled lagging. Momentum z is the family's 20-day mean standardised return divided by a standard error from 500 five-day block-bootstrap draws; from the 2026-11-19 print each family's draws for a day are seeded by the family and the date, so a print moves a past value only when data it depends on change (change log below).
3. **Pattern Integrity (leading candidates).** Fifteen standardised market-state features and a Regime Drift distance per family; descriptive. The nowcast and the per-family sizing multiplier that were to be built on this layer were dropped from the product on 2026-10-28 (change log below); no sizing instruction is published.

## Benchmark-relative performance (descriptive)
**alpha20** compares each live production account with its own family's probe reading. For an account that is active on the reading date and has at least 15 of the previous 20 business days active, alpha20 is the mean of its daily z over those 20 business days (the same per-account z the Scoreboard uses: daily return on start balance over its trailing standard deviation, clipped at ±6) minus the family's probe reading z on the same date, with **beta = 1.0** for every family (fixed, not fitted to any data). The page shows, per family, the reading, the number of accounts scored, the median 20-day account z and median alpha20, and the share of accounts above the benchmark (alpha20 above zero); individual accounts are not published. This is a comparison, not a forecast and not a sizing instruction (T2, T5 and T6 found no forecast, overlay or sizing value in the readings), and the two sides are standardised differently (a daily account z against a two-year z of per-trade R), so alpha20 is not a measured excess return. Descriptive only; no statistical claim is made from it.

## External panel (Collective2, descriptive)
**External panel.** A descriptive panel of third-party algorithmic strategies on Collective2, read through its public General API (read-only, one request per second; nothing is subscribed, copied or traded). The records are hypothetical model accounts with simulated fills and the default commission plan. Universe, fixed in a pre-registration dated 2026-11-04 before any panel data was read (`aci/phase1/step5/C2_PANEL_PREREG_2026-11-04.md`): every forex or futures strategy that existed on 2022-01-03 (dead ones included) or is live and was created before 2026-01-01, with at least 250 observed days. Daily return: the change in each strategy's own end-of-day model-account equity, net of the default commission plan (one snapshot a day, labelled to the broker day that has just closed at 17:00 ET; weekends compound into Monday). Each strategy's return is standardised by its own trailing 120-day standard deviation (clipped at ±6); a family's return is the equal-weight mean of its members with at least five members that day. Families are tagged from each strategy's closed trades (the spread of trade durations and of opening times, how long losers are held against winners, the payoff ratio) with panel-internal terciles fixed at the first build; each strategy is tagged once and the tag is never revised. A second tag from the strategy descriptions is reported beside it in the report; the two sources disagree (of 21 strategies tagged by both, agreement 0.19, Cohen's kappa -0.06), and most descriptions say nothing about mechanism (44 of the members get a description tag), so the tags cannot be checked against the text.
The card shows, per family, the members on the as-of day, the 20-day mean of the standardised family return, its percentile against its own two-year history, and the T1-external result. **T1-external** asks whether the probe family index co-moves with outside algos of the same family: Spearman correlation at the 20-day horizon on day blocks, a 90% block-bootstrap interval, a +40-day shift twin and a tag-shuffle control (labels permuted across the panel's members, 200 draws). A family passes with rho of at least 0.40, the interval above zero, the twin failing and the shuffle 95th percentile below rho; FAIL needs at least 40 blocks, fewer is INCONCLUSIVE. ## Rules that apply to every number here
Pre-registration before any test; a shift-control twin beside every statistic; day-block statistics; realised drawdown only; costs as published; probe results labelled hypothetical; negatives published; backfill drawn differently from the live record; methodology versioned with 30-day notice and 90-day overlap for major changes.

## Change log — newest day booked only from closed trades (effective with the 2026-11-19 print)
A change to how the newest day of each probe symbol is computed. Old rule: the tick walk closed every trade still open at the end of the data at the last tick and booked that provisional result on the last day of data. When the next day's ticks arrived those trades ran on to their real exit, so the last day's value changed: the newest reading was restated every time the data was extended. With a nightly feed that day is the headline, so the headline was restated every night. Example, on all 17 symbols: 2026-11-04 was published by the 2026-11-17 print at TB 0.0585 (MR 0.1179, SC 0.0923) and restated by the 2026-11-18 print to 0.0127 (0.1133, 0.0924).
New rule: a trade still open at the end of the data is not booked at all until it exits; it is booked on its real exit day once the data reaches that day. The newest day therefore carries only trades that have actually closed, and its value does not move when the next day arrives. Only the newest, unfinished day of each symbol is affected; no reading changes once its trades have closed, and the definition of a reading (20-day R per trade against the random control, two-year z) is unchanged. Check, run before this print on the tick data as of 2026-11-19 06:01 ET: computed on identical data under the old and the new rule, the history differs on one row only, 2026-11-10, the last data day of six symbols (TB −0.010, MR −0.002, SC +0.004); every other row is byte-identical. Against the previous print, every row before 2026-11-02 is byte-identical except 146 rows of 2026 that moved by 0.0001 (the fourth decimal), in the readings and the cost regime only. That is the standing revision from the yearly median spread used for costs, which is recomputed as the year's data grows; no row before 2026-01-01 changed. Addendum (published with the 2026-11-20 print): the 2026-11-19 print itself ran on four to seven more data days per symbol than that check, and the same revision was larger. Against the 2026-11-18 print, 204 rows of 2026 before 2026-11-02 moved: trend readings by at most 0.0006, mean-reversion readings by at most 0.0029, short-cycle readings by at most 0.0002, the cost regime by at most 0.0002 (median move of a changed value 0.0002). The larger moves come in runs of 20 days, because a changed spread median can decide whether a stop was touched, so a few trades exit at a different time and every 20-day reading that contains them moves. No row before 2026-01-01 changed. This revision stays until the cost rule stops using the whole year's data; that change is an open decision.

## Change log — Scoreboard bootstrap seeded by family and date (effective with the 2026-11-19 print)
Correction of a defect, approved 2026-11-19. Until this print the Scoreboard drew all its bootstrap samples from one random generator, seeded once and used in order: trend, then mean-reversion, then session-clock, day by day. Each day the trend family gained shifted every later draw, so the whole mean-reversion and session-clock history was redrawn on most prints with no change in the data. On the 2026-11-17 and 2026-11-18 prints, 160 and 154 of 233 mean-reversion percentiles before 2026-11-02 moved, and 92 and 88 of 170 session-clock ones: about two-thirds and half, by a median of 1.3 percentile points and at most 10. Trend never moved, but only because it was drawn first: its stability was an artifact of that order, not evidence that its values were fixed. From this print each family's draws for a day come from a generator seeded by the family and the date, so a day's standard error depends only on its own 120-day window.

One-time rewrite. The new seeds give every day new draws once, trend included. On the account data through 2026-11-18, against the old code, percentiles changed on: trend 60 of 96 days (median 1.8, at most 15.4 points; trend has the shortest history, so its percentile moves in larger steps); mean-reversion 170 of 247 (median 1.3, at most 8.0); session-clock 100 of 183 (median 1.0, at most 7.9). Only momentum z, its standard error (by a median of 2–3 %) and the percentile change. Breadth, drawdown load, kill counts and the per-account z behind benchmark-relative performance and the episodes study are unchanged, and no validation result used the bootstrap (T5 read the kill counts).

Checks run before this print, on a copy: two runs on the same data are byte-identical, and cutting the account data one or four business days short changes no earlier row (the old code changed 285 mean-reversion and 221 session-clock rows on the one-day cut). The rule from this print: a past Scoreboard value moves only when the account data behind it change, through a restated account history, a changed family tag or a new account. A new account enters its family from its first day once it has twenty trading days, because its first volatility estimate is applied back to that day, so the latest four weeks can move when new accounts start; nothing older moves for that reason.

## Change log — closed-day rule and nightly tick feed (effective with the 2026-11-16 print)
Two operational changes, no change of method. First, the Scoreboard now prints only a closed broker day: a broker day (broker clock = New York + 7 hours) closes at 17:00 New York time, and a day still open at print time, which could be carried by a part-day panel, is not printed; the earlier rule (a day is printed once at least half of the usual panel has reported) still applies on top of it. Second, the tick files behind the probes and the market layer are no longer re-downloaded by hand: each night the closed day's ticks are appended from the same public source as the earlier files, after a check against the existing file (identical timestamps and prices on every tick compared); the existing history is not rewritten. Readings therefore advance as the feed catches up and then each night, and print on the day they are measured once it has. First feed night (2026-11-16 print): the probe family readings advance to 2026-11-02, a late publication; the 25-symbol Layer 2 reading still ends 2026-10-30 until every symbol has caught up. The existing ticks are not rewritten, but extending the data moves the full-sample scale constants and the end-of-data handling of the probe walk, so values already published for 2026-01-02 to 2026-10-30 were revised with this print: probe family readings by at most 0.0023, the cost regime by at most 0.0001, drift values by at most 0.009; nothing before 2026-01-02 changed. The feed's ticks match the earlier files tick for tick on the days compared but carry about 1.8 percent more ticks on the same day (EURUSD, 2026-10-30), which can move the tick-count feature slightly.

## Change log — sizing multiplier dropped (decision 2026-10-28, effective with the 2026-10-28 print)
The roadmap carried a third output, a per-family sizing multiplier (0.5–1.5, banded, with a dwell), to be published once a nowcast test (T2) and a sizing test (T3) had passed. It was never published. Four pre-registered tests have now returned the same answer from different directions: T2 found no out-of-sample forecast value in the market-state features; T5 found that overlays on the raw readings do not beat same-window permutation nulls on 105 strategy curves and 150 live accounts; T6B found no return persistence at 5–20 days in the family panels once account composition is controlled; T6C found the best of 38 sizing rules per family inside a search-aware permutation null in every window. The operator decided on 2026-10-28 to drop the multiplier from the product rather than keep it on the roadmap. From this print the index publishes no sizing instruction; the JSON field `multipliers` states that it is dropped and can no longer publish; T3 is retired and T2's record stands. The only risk outputs are the Scoreboard's realised drawdown load and SPRT kill lines, which are coincident, account-level and lagging by construction. The probe index, the Scoreboard and the Pattern Integrity features are unchanged. Should a future pre-registered test establish a sizing mechanism, it would enter as a new output under the 30-day notice and 90-day overlap rule, not by reviving this one.

## Change log — mean-reversion family audit (2026-10-28, caveat effective with the 2026-10-28 print)
The T6 finding that the mean-reversion reading moves opposite to the library mean-reversion strategies was audited the same day under a pre-registration (`aci/phase4/mr_audit`). Decomposing the published reading shows that the mean-reversion probes' own per-trade results track neither the library mean-reversion pool nor the live mean-reversion panel (rho −0.09 to +0.08); the random control they are read against does track the library pool (+0.20 to +0.38), so the published difference carried the control's cost-regime signal with the sign flipped. Re-walking the same entries with fixed 3-, 6- and 12-hour exits (one tick walk per geometry, random control matched) did not reach the pre-registered bar either: best tracking of the library pool +0.26 on 2025–26 and +0.15 on the K-cycle lockbox against a bar of +0.40. By the pre-registered rules neither hypothesis is confirmed, so the mean-reversion reading stays v0.2 and is published with the caveat "does not represent the library mean-reversion family; descriptive"; the family is not split; the next look is the 2027 live record. A third, descriptive step (splits of the same record) found the opposite movement concentrated in the K-cycle library strategies and in the legs of the fourteen non-K-cycle probe symbols, and not distinguishable from zero against the other library strategies or against the live mean-reversion accounts once account composition is removed; it changes nothing above. Nothing was chosen by result and no sign is flipped. The trend and session-clock readings are unaffected.
